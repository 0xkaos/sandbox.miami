    Getting a useful result

    Use a desktop or laptop, with the camera near the screen. Light your face from the front, avoid glare on glasses, and sit at a comfortable, consistent distance.

    Choose full screen before calibration. Start with 9 points, check accuracy, then compare 17 points or the curved mapping. Recalibrate after moving your camera, seat, or window.

    This is an experimental estimate. Expect broad areas to be easier than small buttons. Iris tracking alone does not know where you are looking; your calibration learns that mapping.

    Camera won’t start?

    A browser can fail before showing a permission prompt. If Zoom works in the same browser, compare the selected camera and check this site’s camera permission. Choose Default camera, or the built-in color camera if both color and infrared cameras appear.

    Otherwise, check your laptop’s camera privacy switch or shutter and whether the camera works in your system’s Camera app. Close that app before retrying here.

    On Windows, check Settings → Privacy & security → Camera, including access for desktop apps such as your browser. On Linux, check that the camera is enabled and that your browser package has camera/device access.

    Stop and start the camera here to reconnect. For your Microsoft LifeCam on Linux, reset it in a terminal if needed:

    sudo usbreset 045e:0811

    Then click Start camera again. A webpage cannot reset a USB device. HTTPS or localhost is required.
